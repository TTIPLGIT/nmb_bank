Credit Risk Management

Risk Analysis & Lending Control Module

1. Credit risk management is the process of identifying, assessing, and mitigating the risk of borrower default. It ensures financial institutions maintain asset quality and financial stability.

2. Banks evaluate borrower creditworthiness using financial statements, repayment history, and credit bureau reports before approving loans.

3. Probability of Default (PD) measures the likelihood that a borrower will fail to meet debt obligations within a specified period.

4. Loss Given Default (LGD) estimates the percentage of exposure that a bank may lose if a borrower defaults.

5. Exposure at Default (EAD) represents the total value a bank is exposed to at the time of default.

6. Non-Performing Assets (NPA) are loans where interest or principal payments are overdue beyond regulatory thresholds.

7. Collateral reduces credit risk by providing security against loan exposure.

8. Basel III norms require banks to maintain adequate capital buffers against credit risk.

9. Provisioning ensures that banks set aside funds to cover potential loan losses.

10. Effective credit monitoring and early warning systems help detect stress signals and prevent major defaults.
